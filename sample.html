Worked Example

A full solution in GRESA format, with a Substitution step and a Scientific Conclusion.

A communications satellite of mass $m = 1000\ \text{kg}$ is in a circular parking orbit at radius $r_1 = 7.0 \times 10^6\ \text{m}$ from Earth's center. Boss Man wants to move it to a higher circular operational orbit at radius $r_2 = 1.4 \times 10^7\ \text{m}$.

Mass $1000\ \text{kg}$
Parking radius $7.0 \times 10^6\ \text{m}$
Operational radius $1.4 \times 10^7\ \text{m}$
Gravitational constant $6.67 \times 10^{-11}$
Earth mass $5.97 \times 10^{24}\ \text{kg}$
a Calculate the total mechanical energy $U_1$ of the satellite in the parking orbit and the total mechanical energy $U_2$ in the operational orbit.
Given

$m = 1000\ \text{kg}$, $r_1 = 7.0 \times 10^6\ \text{m}$, $r_2 = 1.4 \times 10^7\ \text{m}$

$G = 6.67 \times 10^{-11}\ \text{N}\cdot\text{m}^2/\text{kg}^2$, $M_E = 5.97 \times 10^{24}\ \text{kg}$

Required

$U_1$ and $U_2$

Equation

For a circular orbit, the total mechanical energy is

$$U = -\frac{G M_E m}{2r}$$

Substitution

$$U_1 = -\frac{(6.67 \times 10^{-11})(5.97 \times 10^{24})(1000)}{2(7.0 \times 10^6)}$$

$$U_2 = -\frac{(6.67 \times 10^{-11})(5.97 \times 10^{24})(1000)}{2(1.4 \times 10^7)}$$

Solution

$$G M_E m \approx 3.98 \times 10^{17}\ \text{J}\cdot\text{m}$$

$$U_1 = -\frac{3.98 \times 10^{17}}{1.4 \times 10^7} \approx -2.84 \times 10^{10}\ \text{J}$$

$$U_2 = -\frac{3.98 \times 10^{17}}{2.8 \times 10^7} \approx -1.42 \times 10^{10}\ \text{J}$$

Answer

$U_1 \approx -2.84 \times 10^{10}\ \text{J}$ and $U_2 \approx -1.42 \times 10^{10}\ \text{J}$.

b Find the change in total energy $\Delta U = U_2 - U_1$. Is energy added to or removed from the satellite during the orbit change?
Given

$U_1 \approx -2.84 \times 10^{10}\ \text{J}$ and $U_2 \approx -1.42 \times 10^{10}\ \text{J}$ from part (a)

Required

$\Delta U$ and whether energy is added or removed

Equation

$$\Delta U = U_2 - U_1$$

Substitution

$$\Delta U = (-1.42 \times 10^{10}) - (-2.84 \times 10^{10})$$

Solution

$$\Delta U = -1.42 \times 10^{10} + 2.84 \times 10^{10} = +1.42 \times 10^{10}\ \text{J}$$

Because $\Delta U$ is positive, energy is added to the satellite.

Answer

$\Delta U \approx +1.42 \times 10^{10}\ \text{J}$. Energy is added to the satellite.

c As the satellite moves from $r_1$ to $r_2$, explain what happens to its kinetic energy and its gravitational potential energy.
Given

The satellite moves from $r_1 = 7.0 \times 10^6\ \text{m}$ to a larger radius $r_2 = 1.4 \times 10^7\ \text{m}$.

Required

The direction of change of kinetic energy ($KE$) and gravitational potential energy ($PE$).

Equation

$$KE = \frac{G M_E m}{2r}, \qquad PE = -\frac{G M_E m}{r}$$

Substitution

At $r_1$: $KE_1 \approx 2.84 \times 10^{10}\ \text{J}$, $PE_1 \approx -5.69 \times 10^{10}\ \text{J}$

At $r_2$: $KE_2 \approx 1.42 \times 10^{10}\ \text{J}$, $PE_2 \approx -2.84 \times 10^{10}\ \text{J}$

Solution

Because $r$ appears in the denominator, larger $r$ means smaller $1/r$. So $KE$ decreases from $2.84 \times 10^{10}\ \text{J}$ to $1.42 \times 10^{10}\ \text{J}$.

$PE$ is negative, and its magnitude decreases as $r$ increases. So $PE$ becomes less negative, from $-5.69 \times 10^{10}\ \text{J}$ to $-2.84 \times 10^{10}\ \text{J}$. In other words, $PE$ increases.

Answer

Kinetic energy decreases. Gravitational potential energy increases (becomes less negative).

Scientific Conclusion

Moving a satellite from a lower circular orbit to a higher one requires energy to be added to the system. In this example, the total mechanical energy increases from approximately $-2.84 \times 10^{10}\ \text{J}$ to $-1.42 \times 10^{10}\ \text{J}$, a net gain of about $1.42 \times 10^{10}\ \text{J}$. This energy is supplied by the rocket engine's burns.

Physically, the satellite slows down in the higher orbit, so its kinetic energy decreases. However, its gravitational potential energy becomes less negative because it is farther from Earth. The increase in potential energy is larger than the decrease in kinetic energy, which is why the total mechanical energy rises. This is a direct consequence of the total mechanical energy of a bound orbit being negative: as the radius grows, $U$ approaches zero from below, so $U$ increases even though the satellite itself slows down.
